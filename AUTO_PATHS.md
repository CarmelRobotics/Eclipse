## Confirmed configuration and auto-only power

The team's PathPlanner settings remain 43 kg, MOI 4 kg m^2, COF 2.255, 7.03 drive
reduction, 0.052 m wheel radius, and 91 A planner current. Robot TunerConstants
now also uses the confirmed physical 7.03 reduction, fixing the former 5.273
odometry/velocity-conversion mismatch.

DriveCurrentPolicy is independent of the optional POWER_MANAGER flag:

- Enabled autonomous: request 91 A SUPPLY per drive motor.
- Teleop, disabled, test: request 40 A supply.
- Below 8.0 V, or invalid voltage: reduce to 40 A.
- Recover the boost only after at least 0.5 s continuously at/above 9.5 V.
- Preserve the separate 120 A STATOR ceiling.
- Set the timed lower limit explicitly: lower time is 0 (disabled), and lower
  current matches the selected 91/40 A cap. Phoenix's default 40 A after 1 s of
  current limiting must not silently override the team's requested auto policy.
  This removes that timed breaker-protection reduction; the 8 V software fallback
  is not equivalent protection.
- Recheck mode/voltage on the CAN worker before each motor write, so a stale queued
  boost cannot reapply 91 A after auto. Requests are coalesced onto one worker.
- Failed drive-limit writes are reported and retried at 2 Hz. CAN writes remain
  asynchronous: restoration is requested immediately, not guaranteed instantaneous.

Optional flywheel budgets remain feature-gated. Their values are unchanged.
Drive supply no longer follows the optional teleop sprint/scoring budgets, because
the team explicitly requested 40 A outside auto.

91 A per drive motor is up to 364 A for drive alone. Voltage fallback is NOT a
battery/breaker safety guarantee; CAN/config/20 ms reaction cannot prevent every sag.
Physical battery, connector, breaker, and temperature checks are still required.

The stored 6 m/s max-speed setting was not changed. With 7.03 gearing and ~2.05-inch
Kraken X60 wheels, ideal 12 V free speed is only about 4.65 m/s; true loaded speed
must be measured. Current generated opening peak is capped at 4.60 m/s, below that
free-speed bound. Do not interpret the stored 6 m/s number as measured performance.
